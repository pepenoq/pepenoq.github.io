Four 25-question sets (100 questions total) covering NUR 504 Exam 3 — Week 7 (Burns) and Week 8 (Shock, Sepsis and DIC).

Sets 1 and 2 are burn injury — classifying depth from the findings and the rule that superficial and full-thickness burns never blister, the rule of nines, the Parkland formula and the fact that its 8-hour clock starts at the time of injury, mean arterial pressure, electrical injury with its dysrhythmias and iceberg effect, myoglobin and acute kidney injury, inhalation injury and the airway priority, the capillary leak that drives hypovolemia, the potassium and sodium course, compartment syndrome and escharotomy, paralytic ileus and Curling’s ulcer, burn wound sepsis, and the emergent, acute and rehabilitative phases. Sets 3 and 4 are shock and sepsis — why perfusion fails before the blood pressure does, oxygen delivery and demand, the lactate trend, compensation and the four stages through refractory, the hypovolemic, cardiogenic, obstructive and distributive states with the neurogenic and anaphylactic exceptions, SIRS criteria and screening, the hour-1 bundle, MODS, DIC, and the vasoactive drugs from norepinephrine to dobutamine.

Four case-study chains run across the sets, where several consecutive questions follow one evolving patient: a house fire, a circumferential burn, a bee-sting anaphylaxis and a urinary tract infection that becomes sepsis.

Every item is drawn only from the Exam 3 material — the Week 7 and Week 8 objectives, the lecture decks and their speaker notes, and the in-class activities and case studies. Following the course question prompt, every question is written at the Application level or higher: it asks what the nurse should do, conclude or prioritize, never what a term means. Each rationale explains why the correct answer is correct and lists why every incorrect option is not the best answer, then names the Bloom’s level. Progress saves automatically in your browser.
