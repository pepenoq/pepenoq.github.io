Four 25-question sets (100 questions total) covering the whole of NUR 525 Exam 2 — Week 4, Week 5, and Week 6.

Set 1 is the US health care system, insurance and policy — why care here costs what it does, how cost and quality actually relate, the Affordable Care Act, premium versus deductible versus copay versus coinsurance, Medicare Parts A through D and what Medicare will not pay for, Medicaid and the coverage gap, COBRA after a job loss, EMTALA, who faces barriers to care, the funded programs from WIC to CHIP, and how the local, state and federal levels partner. Set 2 is epidemiology and surveillance — the epidemiologic triangle and how to break a leg of it, descriptive versus analytic epidemiology, incidence versus prevalence, endemic/epidemic/pandemic, passive versus active versus syndromic surveillance, notifiable and quarantinable disease, vectors, and active versus passive immunity. Set 3 is sexually transmitted infections and hepatitis — what a vaccine prevents, what an antibiotic cures and what only an antiviral manages, the HIV window period and PEP, syphilis staging and the conversations it requires, and hepatitis A, B and C. Set 4 is tuberculosis, foodborne illness and communicable disease control — latent versus active disease, reading a skin test against the 5, 10 and 15 mm thresholds, the drug regimens and their teaching, directly observed therapy, airborne precautions, and food safety.

Every item is drawn only from the Exam 2 material — the Week 4, 5 and 6 objectives and the three Are You Ready? worksheets — and every objective in those files is represented. Questions are written as hard NCLEX critical-thinking items, multiple choice and select-all-that-apply, with no filler options: each distractor is a real concept, level of prevention, ethical principle, agency or intervention borrowed from an adjacent topic. Each rationale explains why the right answer is right and why the tempting wrong one is not, and names the Bloom’s level. Following the course material, no question requires any calculation.
